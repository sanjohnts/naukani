Nature & Conservation

Retaining the existing natural landscape, developing a natural forest environment and helping people understand why biodiversity matters.

Environmental concept

Nature at the centre

The Nadukani project has been described as retaining the existing natural landscape and developing a natural forest environment, rather than replacing it.

That idea shapes how this website approaches conservation: celebrating native plants and wildlife, explaining why habitats matter, and encouraging every visitor to experience nature responsibly.

Themes

What conservation means here

The themes at the heart of this website's educational content.

Wildlife conservation awarenessUnderstanding the threats animals face and how people can help protect them.
Biodiversity educationLearning how species, habitats and ecosystems depend on one another.
Natural habitat exhibitsAnimal spaces designed around naturalistic environments.
Native plant lifeProtecting and appreciating the plants of Kerala and the Western Ghats.
Education for studentsNature learning for schools, colleges and young visitors.
Responsible wildlife tourismVisiting in ways that respect animals, people and the landscape.
Rainwater conservationRainwater harvesting and water management, part of earlier project descriptions.

Plans, not programmes. These are the project's stated plans and the themes this website focuses on. Conservation and education programmes will be listed here only once they are officially launched.

Why it matters

Forests, water & life

Forests hold soil and water, cool the land around them and shelter countless species. In a monsoon landscape like Kerala's, capturing rainwater and protecting green cover go hand in hand.

  • Healthy forests protect soil and water
  • Native plants support native wildlife
  • Every visitor can learn to tread lightly
